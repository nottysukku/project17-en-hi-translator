Project 17 — STM32N6570-DK 3.5 mm jack → PAM8403 → 4 Ω 3 W speaker · NUCLEO-WBA55CG music in through a TMUX1574

One speaker plays both: the N6's spoken Hindi and the WBA's Bluetooth LE Audio music. The WBA sends its decoded music as I2S to the DK, through the TMUX1574, and the DK plays whichever source has the floor on its own 3.5 mm jack (WM8904 codec). Music always wins. The TMUX keeps the two boards apart until the DK has set its pins to receive, and whenever the DK is off. No MAX98357A. No battery: the PAM8403 runs from the DK's 5 V pin, and each board from its own USB cable.

Before you wire: flash the new firmware first

The old firmware drives A2, D6 and D8 as outputs (it sent the Hindi voice out on them) and drives D7 for the old SEL. With the WBA connected, both boards would then drive the same lines. Flash the firmware from branch claude/peaceful-mccarthy-gdlqoe (commit "Translator audio: Hindi voice and WBA music both out of the DK's 3.5 mm jack" or later) before steps 3 to 11. The new firmware makes A2, D6 and D8 inputs and only then pulls D7 low; until then the 10 kΩ pull-up keeps the TMUX open.

Wire it in this order

Numbers match the red markers on the drawing. Keep both USB cables unplugged while wiring, and check the grounds, the 5 V line and the capacitor's polarity before you power up. The TMUX1574PWR is a TSSOP-16 chip: solder it on a TSSOP-16 to DIP adapter first (0.65 mm pitch), pin 1 at the dot.

1+3V3 → TMUX VDD

DK CN8 pin 4 → TMUX pin 16. Solder a 100 nF ceramic from pin 16 to pin 8, right at the chip.

2Common ground

Join DK CN8 pin 6, TMUX pin 8, PAM8403 GND and Nucleo CN5 pin 6. Also tie TMUX pin 1 (SEL: always side A) and the unused B side, pins 3, 6, 10, 13, to ground. Every ⏚ is this one net.

3D7 = PD6 → EN

DK CN11 pin 8 → TMUX pin 15, and 10 kΩ from pin 15 to +3V3 (pin 16). EN is active low: the pull-up keeps the switch open until the firmware pulls D7 low. With the DK off, the chip's powered-off protection keeps it open too.

4D1 → A2 = PE12

TMUX pin 4 → DK CN7 pin 3: the WBA's bit clock into SAI2_SCK_B, AF10. 48 kHz × 32 bits = 1.536 MHz. Keep the three I2S wires short.

5D2 → D6 = PE13

TMUX pin 7 → DK CN11 pin 7: word select into SAI2_FS_B. Low = left slot, high = right slot.

6D3 → D8 = PE7

TMUX pin 9 → DK CN12 pin 1: data into SAI2_SD_B, I2S Philips, 16-bit. The DK mixes left and right into one channel.

7D4 → D2 = PD0

TMUX pin 12 → DK CN11 pin 3, with 100 kΩ to ground at the DK: the "music playing" line. With the switch open it reads low: no music.

8PB6 → S1A (dashed)

WBA bit clock, SAI1_SCK_B. Nucleo CN8 pin 4 ("D3") → TMUX pin 2. PB6 is also user button B2.

9PB5 → S2A (dashed)

WBA word select, SAI1_FS_B. Nucleo morpho CN4 pin 35 → TMUX pin 5. Arduino D1 would need a solder bridge, so use the morpho pin.

10PB7 → S3A (dashed)

WBA data, SAI1_SD_B. Nucleo CN8 pin 3 ("D2") → TMUX pin 11. PB7 is also user button B3. ST's own LE Audio example uses these three pins.

11PB0 → S4A (dashed)

Nucleo CN8 pin 7 ("D6") → TMUX pin 14. The WBA holds it high while music plays; the DK then plays the music and the translator pauses.

12+5V, GND, 470 µF → PAM8403

DK CN8 pin 5 (+5 V) → PAM8403 +5V, ground → its GND. Solder the 470 µF electrolytic (16 V or more) straight across the module's +5V and GND pins: the long leg / unmarked side to +5V, the stripe to GND. It feeds the amplifier's current peaks so the DK's 5 V does not dip.

13Jack → PAM8403 input

A 3.5 mm plug into the DK's audio jack, its wires to the PAM8403 input: tip → L, ring → R, sleeve → G (input ground). Both channels carry the same signal. Keep this cable away from the speaker wires.

14Speaker 4 Ω · 3 W

PAM8403 L+ and L− → the speaker's two terminals. The outputs are bridge-tied: never connect a speaker wire to ground, to the other channel or to the USB shield. A second 4 Ω speaker may go on R+ / R−.

What the speaker plays

  • AUTO (default): the music while the WBA's line is high (after 0.1 s), the Hindi voice otherwise (1.5 s after the music stops; two short beeps).
  • Music always wins: speech fades out within 5 ms, the translation stops, and the screen goes dark after 5 s.
  • TRANSLATE and MUSIC force one source. TONE plays 880 Hz through the jack.

Average power is far below the 3 W rating. If the sound distorts, or the DK resets on loud music, rebuild with -DXL_AUDIO_OUT_VOLUME_DB=-20 or turn the module's knob down if it has one.

Safety and power

  • The PAM8403 drives both terminals of each speaker with fast PWM. Shorting either one to ground, or L to R, can destroy it.
  • The 470 µF capacitor is polarized: the stripe goes to GND. Reversed, it can burst.
  • Power the DK by USB-C on CN6 from a USB-C port or charger. UM3300: the board can then draw 1.66 or 3.2 A. An old USB-A port can limit it to 0.55 A, too little for loud music.
  • The Nucleo runs from its own ST-LINK USB cable. Its ground still joins the common ground.
  • The DK, Nucleo and TMUX signal pins are 3.3 V. Never put 5 V on a header signal pin.

Pins avoided, and why

  • PD12 on the DK is the Ethernet chip's MDIO line, so EN is on D7 = PD6.
  • PA8 on the Nucleo is the ST-LINK virtual COM port's RX line, so the WBA uses SAI1 block B instead of block A.
  • The DK's codec sits on PB0, PB6, PB7, PG7 (SAI1 block A) and I2C2 (shared with the touch screen): on-board, nothing to wire.
  • No MAX98357A any more: the old D4 = PH5 amplifier-shutdown wire is not used.

Signal chain: phone → WBA (LC3 decode) → WBA SAI1_B (PB6 / PB5 / PB7) → TMUX1574 side A (EN = D7, SEL = GND) → DK SAI2_B (A2 / D6 / D8) → DK audio stream ← N6 Hindi voice; → SAI1_A → WM8904 → 3.5 mm jack → PAM8403 (470 µF at its 5 V input) → 4 Ω 3 W speaker. Music line: Nucleo PB0 → TMUX → DK PD0. Power: DK USB-C → CN8 pin 5 (+5 V) → PAM8403; Nucleo on its own USB.
